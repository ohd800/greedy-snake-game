How the conversion works

Octal is base 8, so each position represents a power of 8. To convert from decimal, divide by 8 repeatedly and collect the remainders:

Worked example. Convert 100 to octal:

  • 100 ÷ 8 = 12 remainder 4
  • 12 ÷ 8 = 1 remainder 4
  • 1 ÷ 8 = 0 remainder 1

Reading the remainders from the bottom up gives 144. Checking the other way:

1×8² + 4×8¹ + 4×8⁰ = 64 + 32 + 4 = 100

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert decimal to octal by hand?
Repeatedly divide the number by 8 and keep each remainder. Read the remainders from last to first. For 100: 100/8 = 12 remainder 4, 12/8 = 1 remainder 4, 1/8 = 0 remainder 1, giving 144 in octal.
Why is octal used in computing?
Octal groups binary neatly into sets of three bits, which made it a compact way to read binary on early computers. It is still most visible today in Unix file permissions, where values such as 755 encode read, write and execute rights.
Can this tool handle negative numbers?
Yes. The sign is preserved and applied to the converted value, so −100 decimal becomes −144 octal.
What about numbers too large for JavaScript?
Values beyond about 15 digits lose precision because JavaScript numbers are double-precision floats. For everyday use this is not a problem, but for cryptographic-size integers use a big-integer library instead.
Is my data uploaded anywhere?
No. The conversion runs entirely in your browser; nothing is sent to a server.
