The formula behind these numbers

Equal temperament divides the octave into twelve equal semitones, so one semitone is a ratio of the twelfth root of two. With A4 (MIDI 69) fixed at 440 Hz, the frequency of any MIDI note m is:

f = 440 × 2(m − 69) / 12

Two useful consequences: moving up one octave doubles the frequency, and moving up one semitone multiplies it by about 1.0595 (roughly 5.95%).

Frequently asked questions

What frequency is A4?
A4 is 440 Hz. It is the international tuning reference (ISO 16) and MIDI note 69. You can read more on the A4 = 440 Hz page.
What is the frequency range of an 88-key piano?
A standard 88-key piano runs from A0 at 27.50 Hz to C8 at 4186.01 Hz.
What is middle C in Hz?
Middle C (C4, MIDI 60) is 261.63 Hz. Note that some manufacturers, Yamaha among them, label this note C3 — the sound is identical, only the octave numbering differs.
How do you calculate a note frequency from a MIDI number?
Use f = 440 × 2^((m − 69) / 12), where m is the MIDI note number. MIDI 69 is A4 = 440 Hz, MIDI 60 is middle C, and each increase of 12 doubles the frequency.
Do these frequencies change between instruments?
The nominal frequencies do not, but real instruments vary. Pianos are stretched-tuned slightly, orchestras sometimes tune to 442 or 443 Hz rather than 440, and wind players bend pitch by design. The table gives the theoretical equal-temperament values.
